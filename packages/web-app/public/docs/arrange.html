Chapter 8

Arranging

Turn loops into a song by painting when each track plays.

Painting clips

Press {app+a}. The arrangement replaces the editor: one row per track, bars along the top. Press it again to go back to the code.

  • The first time, every track is filled from start to end, so nothing changes until you carve.
  • Drag on a row to paint a clip. A track plays only inside its clips, and an empty row is silent.
  • A track you add later arrives filled, so arranging never quietly drops a part.
  • The song ends at the end of its last clip (the dashed line), and playback stops there.

Inside a clip, the pattern starts where the clip starts: a <a b> begins on a in every clip. Splitting or trimming a clip changes nothing you hear; a clip that enters its pattern partway shows ▸bar after its title, and right-click → enter at bar 1 resets it.

Double-click a clip to jump to its block in the code. Right-click for rename… ({mod+r}), which renames the track everywhere, and color….

Notes inside clips

A track written with clips() takes its notes from the clips themselves: each clip holds its own, and double-clicking one draws in it. Copies share their notes until you choose make unique in the clip's menu. 

lead: clips().synth("Wavetable")
lead: clips().synth("Serum 2")

Loop regions

Drag across the loops strip above the bars to mark a region and name it. Playback loops there every time it gets there. {app+l} (from the code, too) lets go of the loop you are in, and playback runs on to the next region or to the end. Stopping arms them all again. A new arrangement starts with one region over the whole song, so it loops until you remove it (right-click a region to rename or remove it).

Automation

auto("sweep") is a lane drawn against the song's bars, readable anywhere a signal goes. Double-click auto to draw it under the clips. 

pad: note("<[c3,eb3,g3] [ab2,c3,eb3]>").synth("Wavetable")
  .fx("Filter").param("Cutoff", auto("sweep").range(0.2, 0.8))

The lane box names the lane you are drawing on: type to rename, ▾ to search or create. Pin several to read them against each other; a span copied from one lane ({mod+c}) pastes into another. Which lanes are pinned is saved with the song.

Groups

Select some tracks and press {mod+g}. They are wrapped in a group, which mixes them into one channel: 

drums: group({
  kick: s("pt_kit:0*4")
  snare: s("~ pt_kit:1 ~ pt_kit:1")
  hats: s("pt_kit:4*8").vel("1 .5")
}).fx("Reverb").param("Mix", 0.15).postgain(0.8)
drums: group({
  kick: s("bd*4")
  snare: s("~ sd ~ sd")
  hats: s("hh*8").vel("1 .5")
}).postgain(0.8)
  • The braces are the membership: where a track is written is where it belongs.
  • An .fx(), .postgain() or .bus() on the group applies to all of it, and the mixer shows it as one strip that unfolds.
  • Groups nest. A bare main: group() receives every ungrouped track: that's where a mastering chain goes.
  • _drums: mutes the lot, Sdrums: solos it. A marker on one member reaches only that member.
  • The caret beside the group's line folds it away, in the code and in the arrangement (where its members' clips then draw on its row).
  • A group's own row can be painted too: its clips bring the whole group in and out.
  • Right-click a group to ungroup it, or a member to take it out.

Within a loop, <a b>, .when() and .preset("<a b>") vary a part. A second track in a group is for a section: a fill, an outro. copy("kick") duplicates a whole track (kick2: copy("kick").fast(2)); pcopy("kick") takes only its notes.
