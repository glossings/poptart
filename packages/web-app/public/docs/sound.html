Chapter 4

Instruments & effects

Load a sound, chain effects after it, set any control.

The chain

.synth("…") loads an instrument; each .fx("…") adds an effect after it, as many as you like. 

lead: note("c4 e4 g4 b4")
  .synth("Wavetable")
  .fx("Chorus")
  .fx("Delay").postgain(1)

These devices are built in, the same as in the browser build. The sounds tab lists them, and names autocomplete inside the quotes. Your VST/VST3 plugins load the same way (below).

setscale("E minor")

bells: n("0 4 7 <9 11>").sc(4).synth("Rings")
  .param("Model", "'Sympathetic String'").param("Brightness", 0.6)
  .fx("GrainEcho").postgain(0.6)
pulse: note("e1*8").synth("Braids").param("Shape", "'Saw Sub'")
  .fx("Filter").param("Mode", "ladder").param("Cutoff", saw(0.5).range(0.25, 0.55))
  .fx("Overdrive").postgain(0.3)

Filter's circuit modes are each an analog filter's own equations, so they saturate as Drive goes up and their resonance behaves the way the circuit's does. ladder is the transistor four-pole: it loses bass as the resonance rises and sings at the top. diode is the diode four-pole of acid bass lines: rubbery, its peak moving with the resonance, ringing without ever quite singing. ota is a smoother four-pole that keeps its bass. The two-poles take Morph, lowpass to bandpass to highpass: sallen screams as its clipped feedback bites, statevar is smooth and runs through a notch instead of a band, cmos grinds, polar is hard and harsh, and diodepair has a squelchy peak. 

acid: note("c2 c2 c3 <eb2 g2> c2 bb1 c3 f2").synth("Wavetable")
  .fx("Filter").param("Mode", "diode").param("Resonance", 0.85).param("Drive", 3)
  .param("Cutoff", saw(2).range(0.25, 0.6)).postgain(0.5)

Filterbank and Resonator move their bands' levels with Shape. In scroll mode each band shows what Shape was a moment before the band under it, so a sweep travels up the spectrum; in ripple mode Shape strikes the band at Where and the ripples bounce between the ends. Levels takes one cycle of a pattern, laid across the bands from low to high. 

setscale("D dorian")

pad: n("<0 3> [4 7]").sc(3).synth("Wavetable")
  .fx("Resonator").param("Shape", sine(0.5)).param("Decay", 0.4)
  .param("Levels", "<[1 0 .5 1] [0 1 0 .3]>")
  .postgain(0.5)
kick: s("bd*4")
  .fx("Filterbank").param("Mode", "ripple").param("Where", 0.2)
  .param("Shape", perlin(4)).param("Width", 0.3)
  .postgain(0.5)

Kick plays one hit per note, and the note is the pitch it settles on: f0 to a#0 is a kick, higher notes are toms and tuned bass. Sweep is how many semitones above the note the pitch starts, falling on one fixed curve; Decay is the length and Drive hardens the body. Click is the click's level, Click Tone turns it from a pulse into noise, and Click Length is how long it lasts: shorter is brighter, with less sweep. Tone brightens or softens the whole hit and Tail keeps its belly. A new hit restarts the sweep; the last one fades under it. 

kick: note("a0*4").synth("Kick")
  .param("Sweep", "34st").param("Drive", 0.5).param("Tone", 0.8)
  .decay("<0.4 0.9>").postgain(0.8)

Pitch Env takes a drawn shape in place of the fixed curve: the pitch over the first 400 ms of every hit, drawn on the device's graph in milliseconds and hertz. The curve is drawn above the note, so it moves with whatever note is played; ending it on the bottom line ends the hit on the note. 

kick: note("f0*4").synth("Kick")
  .param("Pitch Env", "drop").decay(0.6).postgain(0.8)

_shape("drop", "0,0.75 0.25,0.45,-2 0.5,0 1,0")

Hat plays one hit per note, and a new hit cuts the last, so a closed hat chokes an open one: pattern .decay() and the long hits are the open ones. Middle C plays its squares at their own pitches; other notes retune them. Source picks the squares: an analog cluster, a thinner one, a dense one that stands in for a recorded cymbal, or two, which is a cowbell. Tone moves the filters from dark to bright, Hold keeps the level up before it drops, Body adds a lower band that rings on after the bright one, Noise blends in white noise, and Lofi gives it the grit of a coarse recording. Warp folds the squares into a denser, clangier metal, Ring makes the bright band whistle, and Filter Sweep starts each hit's filters above or below Tone and brings them back: a zap. Harder hits are louder and brighter and sweep further, and no two hits are quite the same. 

hats: note("c3*8").synth("Hat")
  .decay("<0.07 0.07 0.5 0.07>").vel("<1 0.6 0.8 0.6>")
  .param("Tone", sine(0.25).range(0.7, 0.95))
  .postgain(0.6)

Presets in four characters, each with a closed, an open and a pedal hat: analog, thin, crisp and grainy, with cymbals, a crash, a ride and two cowbells; and zap, whistle, clang, foldcymbal and shaker. 

hats: note("c3*8").synth("Hat").preset("<crispclosed crispclosed crispopen crisppedal>").postgain(0.6)
ride: note("c3*4").synth("Hat").preset("crispride").postgain(0.4)
bell: note("~ c3 ~ c3").synth("Hat").preset("cowbell").postgain(0.5)
zap: note("~ ~ c3 ~").synth("Hat").preset("<zap clang>").postgain(0.5)

Drum plays one hit per note: a struck body and a burst of noise. Material is what is struck, from one resonance (a clave) through a wooden bar (a wood block) to a drum skin (a tom, a conga), and the note is the pitch of the body's lowest mode. Edge is where it is struck: turned up, the upper modes come forward, past the lowest at the top. Decay is how long the body rings, Ring how long its upper modes keep up (all the way is a metal shell), Drop how far above its pitch it starts and Click the stick. Noise mixes the body against noise, up to all noise; Noise Tone is where the noise sits, Noise Length how long it lasts, Noise Tail a quieter second stage eight times as long, and Stutter splits its start into quick bursts, as a clap does. Cutoff is a resonant lowpass over each hit, which Filter Sweep starts above or below and brings back over Filter Decay; FM bends the body with a second sine at FM Ratio times the note, fading over the same time. Drive saturates the whole hit. Hits ring into one another, and note offs are ignored. Each hit keeps the controls it was struck with while it rings, so one track can play a whole kit: a preset or a control that changes per note changes the next hit and leaves the last one ringing. Level follows as the hit plays.

It comes with presets, each at its own pitch on c3. Snares: snare, brightsnare, tightsnare, gritsnare, hisssnare. Claps: clap, bigclap, shortclap. Rims: rim, lowrim, woodrim. Toms: lowtom, midtom, hightom, sweeplow, sweepmid, sweephigh, ringlow, ringmid, ringhigh. Hand drums: conga, bongo, slapbongo, lowbongo, doum, tek, timbale, lowtimbale. Wood: woodblock, clave. Swept and bent: zap, knock, clang, boom, buzz, crunch. A preset sets every control but Level and Voices. 

kit: note("c3*8").synth("Drum")
  .preset("<[sweeplow brightsnare lowrim brightsnare] [slapbongo bigclap woodblock bigclap]>")
zaps: note("c3 [~ c3] [c3 c3] ~").synth("Drum").preset("<zap knock>")
  .param("Filter Decay", "<30ms 80ms 200ms>").postgain(0.6)
wood: note("c3*8").synth("Drum").preset("clave")
  .param("Material", "<0 0.25 0.5 0.75>").postgain(0.4)

Analog is a classic subtractive synth. Each oscillator is a saw, square (with Width), triangle or sine; osc 2 sits Osc 2 Semi and Osc 2 Detune away, Sync restarts it with osc 1 so its tuning moves the tone, and Mix sets the balance of the two. Sub is a square an octave down and Noise is white. Drift lets each oscillator wander slightly out of tune. Every voice has its own filter: Filter Type picks one of the analog circuits the Filter effect has, by the same names, and Morph turns the multimode ones toward bandpass and highpass; Cutoff and Resonance set it, Drive pushes into it, Key Track moves it with the note, and Env Amount is how far the filter envelope sweeps it, up or down, in semitones. Velocity is how much a harder note opens the filter further. Both envelopes are on the device's graphs. With Voices at 1 it is a monosynth: a note played over a held one slides there over Glide without starting the envelopes again, which is how an acid line slides.

Presets: acid, bass, sub, lead, sync (one voice each), pad, strings, brass, keys, pluck. A preset sets every control but Level, and these set Voices too. 

acid: note("c2 c2 [c3 eb2] c2 g1 c2 [bb1 c2] c3").synth("Analog").preset("acid")
  .clip("0.5 0.5 [1.2 0.5] 0.5 1.2 0.5 [1.2 0.5] 0.5")
  .param("Cutoff", sine(0.125).range(0.25, 0.5)).postgain(0.5)
pad: note("<[c3,eb3,g3,bb3] [ab2,c3,eb3,g3]>").synth("Analog").preset("pad").postgain(0.4)

Controls

.param("name", value) sets a control on the last device in the chain, by the name its panel shows. The value can be a number, a pattern, or anything that moves (next chapter). 

keys: n("0 2 4 <5 7>").scale("D dorian").synth("Plaits")
  .param("Engine", "Chord")
  .param("Timbre", sine(0.2))
  .fx("Reverb").param("Mix", 0.35).postgain(0.45)

Every value is a 0..1 position on the control, and the device maps it to its real range (Cutoff runs 20 Hz to 20 kHz, so 0.5 is about 630 Hz). A list control takes its option's name ("ladder", or "'Saw Sub'" for a name with a space in it) or index; a switch takes 0 or 1. So an LFO or a macro drives any control as it is.

A value written with a unit is in the control's own units: "3x", "800hz", "2khz", "-6db", "250ms", "7st", "50ct". A note name on a control in hertz is its frequency. A bare number is a position. 

bell: n("0 2 4 <5 7>").scale("D dorian").synth("FM")
  .param("Op 2 Level", 0.6).param("Mod 2 to 1", 0.5)
  .param("Op 2 Ratio", "<1x 2x 3.5x 7x>")
  .fx("Filter").param("Cutoff", "<1.2khz 4khz>").postgain(0.5)

To find names, evaluate once, then open params in the sounds tab: every control of every device on the track, searchable, click to copy. They autocomplete inside .param("…") too.

Name a device first and .param() reaches it wherever it sits in the chain: .param("Delay", "Feedback", 0.6). When two devices share a name, "Filter#2" is the second of them in chain order. A label given as .fx("Filter", { label: "lo" }) is reached as "lo" however the chain is reordered. .wet() takes a device the same way. Inside a .when(), a control follows the condition, then yields: to a .param() outside the .when(), or, with none, to wherever the control was before. A .param() after the .when() wins over it, as a later call always does. 

keys: n("0 2 4 <5 7>").scale("D dorian").synth("FM")
  .fx("Filter", { label: "lo" }).param("Cutoff", 0.35)
  .fx("Delay").wet(0)
  .when("<0 1>", x => x.param("lo", "Cutoff", 0.7).wet("Delay", 0.5))
  .postgain(1)

.wet(v) after a device blends it with what goes into it: 0 bypasses, 1 (the default) is fully processed. It takes a signal, so an effect can fade in over a song.

Parallel branches

.split() runs the chain in parallel and sums the branches; anything after it processes the sum. Each branch is a function that builds a chain on the signal it is handed, (x) => x is the signal as it arrives, and a branch's .postgain() and .pan() are its own level and place in the sum. Every device in every branch is on the same track, so notes routed in with .midi() strike a gate in any of them, and .param() reaches the device just before it, branch or not. Here two low-pass gates, one short and one long, sit either side of the dry synth: 

lead: note("c2 eb2 g2 <bb2 f2>").synth("Wavetable")
  .split(
    (x) => x.postgain(0.3),
    (b) => b.fx("LPG").midi("lead").param("Decay", 0.08).pan(-0.6),
    (b) => b.fx("LPG").midi("lead").param("Decay", 0.6).pan(0.6).postgain(0.7),
  )
  .fx("Reverb").param("Mix", 0.25).postgain(0.6)

.bands([frequencies], ...) splits by frequency instead, one function per band from the lowest up. The crossovers are Linkwitz-Riley, so bands left alone add back up to the input, and each one is a control: a number, a pattern or a signal, swept here. The devices in the bands number as they would in a .split(): the crossovers are not effects of the chain. 

drums: s("pt_kit:0*4, ~ pt_kit:4 ~ pt_kit:4")
  .bands([sine(0.1).range(150, 400), 2500],
    (low) => low,
    (mid) => mid.fx("Distort").param("Drive", 0.6),
    (high) => high.postgain(0.8),
  )
  .postgain(0.5)

Branches nest, and a .split() can sit inside a band, or a .bands() inside a branch.

Presets

Double-click the synth or fx name to open the device's panel (a plugin's own window, for a plugin). Change anything there and the whole state is captured into the code as a named preset: the call gains .preset("lead"), and the preset is kept in a folded _preset(…) line at the foot of the buffer. There is nothing to press, and the song sounds the same when it is next opened. 

// a different preset each cycle
lead: note("c4 e4 g4 [b4 c5]").synth("Wavetable").preset("<soft buzz>")
  .postgain(0.5)

Preset names pattern like anything else. Double-click preset to pick, make, copy and tweak them. export hands someone one file that plays exactly what you hear; link copies the patch without its presets. A share link carries the presets with the code. A device's preset opens the same in the browser build and the desktop app.

Most devices come with presets of their own, listed in the preset picker in their panel: .synth("Wavetable").preset("pad") plays one, and a preset of yours with the same name wins. Each sets every control but Level and Voices.

Your plugins

Any VST/VST3 instrument or effect you have installed loads by its own name, in the same places a device does. This guide uses "Serum 2", "Diva" and so on as stand-ins: use plugins you have. The sounds tab lists them (press rescan after installing one; click a name to copy it), and names autocomplete inside the quotes. 

lead: note("c4 e4 g4 b4")
  .synth("Serum 2")
  .fx("ValhallaDelay")
  .fx("Pro-Q 3")

Double-click the name to open the plugin's own window. What you change there is captured into a .preset(), as with a device. 

.param("Filter 1 On", 1)
.param("Filter 1 Freq", 0.6)
.param("Frequency#95", 0.3)   // the control at index 95, where two share a name

A plugin's values are 0..1 unless a mapping file in mappings/*.json gives them real units (one ships for Serum 2, so its cutoff takes Hertz: .param("Filter 1 Freq", sine(0.3).range(200, 5000))). When a plugin reuses a name, "Frequency#95" picks one by index; autocomplete shows that form where names collide.
