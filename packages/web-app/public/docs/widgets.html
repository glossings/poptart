Chapter 11

Controls & displays

Sliders, knobs and step grids drawn in the code; scopes, meters and punchcards beside it.

Some calls are drawn in the editor where they are written. A control is a value you move by hand: moving it rewrites the call in the code and is heard at once, without evaluating. A display shows what a track is doing while the song plays. Both appear once the code has been evaluated.

Controls

Call
slider(value, min, max, step)a box slider, dragged sideways; min and max are 0 and 1 unless given
knob(value, min, max, step)a knob, dragged up and down
toggle(1)1 or 0, switched by a click
button()1 while held down, 0 otherwise
xy(x, y)a pad under the line; .x and .y are its two axes, 0 to 1

Shift-drag a slider or a knob for fine steps, or turn the mouse wheel over it. Each control is a pattern, so it goes anywhere a value does: a gain, a device's control, an argument to .range(). Typing a new number into the call moves the control to match. 

setbpm(120)
keys: n("0 2 4 <7 9>").scale("C minor").synth("Wavetable")
  .param("Osc 1 Position", knob(0.3))
  .fx("Filter").param("Cutoff", slider(0.6, 0.2, 0.9))
  .gain(toggle(1)).postgain(0.4)
kick: s("bd*4").postgain(button().range(0.4, 0))
const pad = xy(0.3, 0.6)
chords: note("<[c3,eb3,g3] [ab2,c3,eb3]>").synth("Wavetable")
  .param("Osc 1 Position", pad.x)
  .fx("Filter").param("Cutoff", pad.y)
  .postgain(0.3)

Steps, bars and grids

Call
steps("x . x .")a row of cells, one per step: . is a rest, x plays 1, a number or a word plays as itself
sliders("0.2 0.8", min, max)a bar per step, each its own value
matrix({ bd: "x...", sd: "..x." })a grid with a row per name; a cell plays its row's name
steps(8) · sliders(8) · matrix({ bd: 8, sd: 8 })that many empty steps, bars halfway up, or empty rows, to click into

These three are drawn in place of their call: the cells stand where the text was. Click a cell to switch it, or drag across a row to paint; drag a bar up or down to set it. Each change rewrites the text behind the cells, so undo, copy and paste carry it like any other code. The first click on a count writes the cells out, so steps(8) becomes steps("x . . . . . . .").

Written with nothing inside, steps() and sliders() draw eight cells. A matrix() needs at least one row: without one it plays nothing, stays text and says so in the console. A row the grid can't read is drawn empty and named in the console; a row written as a variable stays text.

Right-click the cells for randomize: as many hits as the row has now, somewhere else, spelled with the row's own tokens (half the row when it has none, or nothing but); every bar of a sliders() at a new height in its range; every row of a matrix() at once. Edit as text opens the call to type into; with the caret in it, show as cells (show as bars for sliders) draws it again. 

setbpm(118)
drums: s(matrix({ bd: "x . . . x . . .", sd: ". . . . x . . .", hh: 8 })).postgain(0.4)
kick: s("bd").struct(steps(8)).postgain(0.3)
hats: s("hh*8").vel(sliders("1 0.4 0.7 0.4 1 0.4 0.7 0.4")).postgain(0.3)

A matrix row's name is what its cells play. Name the rows after drums and it is a drum grid; name them with numbers and it is a note grid, here scale degrees: 

keys: n(matrix({ 0: "x . . . x . . .", 3: ". x . . . x . .", 7: ". . x . . . x .", 9: ". . . x . . . x" }))
  .scale("C minor").synth("Wavetable").postgain(0.4)
bass: n(steps("0 . 3 . 5 . 7 3")).scale("C minor").sub(7).synth("Wavetable")
  .fx("Filter").param("Cutoff", sliders("0.2 0.5 0.8 0.4"))
  .postgain(0.4)

Size

Drag a widget's lower right corner to resize it, in either direction. The size is written into the call when you let go: { width, height }, as in scope({ width: 480, height: 60 }). A knob, a pad, imager() and chroma() stay square and take one { size }.

Widgetwidth and height are
steps · matrixone cell's; { size } sets both
slidersone bar's; { size } sets the height
slider · toggle · buttonthe control's; { size } sets the width
knob · xy · .imager · .chroma{ size }: the side
the other displaysthe display's; { height } alone keeps the usual width
drums: s(matrix({ bd: "x . . . x . . .", hh: "x x x x x x x x" }, { width: 22, height: 14 })).postgain(0.4)
keys: n("0 4 7 4").scale("C minor").synth("Wavetable").gain(slider(0.7, { width: 160 }))
  .postgain(0.4).scope({ width: 480, height: 60 })

Displays on a track

Called on a track, a display draws under its line. It is the same pattern going in and coming out, so it can go anywhere in the chain.

Call
.scope()the waveform
.spectrum()the frequencies, with their level in dB
.waveform()the level over the last cycles, a line per beat
.meter()the level now, beside the call; taller than wide it stands up, with dB marks
.imager()the stereo image: mono straight up, the sides left and right, the correlation below
.punchcard()the pattern's events as they will play, a row per value
.chroma()the notes sounding, on a circle of pitch classes
.plot()a signal's value over time
chords: note("<[c3,e3,g3] [a2,c3,e3]>").synth("Wavetable").postgain(0.3).punchcard().chroma()
bass: note("c2 ~ c2 g1").synth("Wavetable").postgain(0.4).scope().spectrum()
hats: s("hh*8").postgain(0.3).meter({ width: 12, height: 60 })

The options each display takes: { cycles } for how many cycles .waveform(), .punchcard() and .plot() show; { min, max } for the range of .plot() and the dB range of .spectrum(); { scale, window } for .scope()'s gain and its length in milliseconds.

A display of sound reads a tap on the track's output, and only a few tracks are tapped at once. A display past that number draws nothing and says so when you point at it.

Behind the code

Written on a line of its own, a display is drawn behind the code, filling the editor and staying put as the code scrolls. punchcard() and chroma() show every track; scope(), spectrum(), waveform(), meter() and imager() show the whole song's output. Several share the editor in bands, top to bottom in the order they are written. Delete the line and evaluate to take one away. 

setbpm(124)
punchcard()
spectrum()

kick: s("bd*4").postgain(0.4)
hats: s("hh*8").postgain(0.2)
snare: s("~ sd ~ sd").postgain(0.3)
keys: n("<0 3> 5 7 <10 12>").scale("C minor").synth("Wavetable").postgain(0.3)

In DJ mode each deck draws its own, from its own code.

Track colors

.color() sets the color a track is drawn in everywhere: the arrangement, the mixer, the displays. It takes a CSS color name or a hex color and changes nothing about the sound. A track without one takes a color from the theme, picked by its name; tracks in a group take shades of the group's color.

Click the swatch before the word color, or double-click the word, to open the color picker. Its palette tab suggests colors that go with the rest of the song. 

punchcard()

kick: s("bd*4").color('tomato').postgain(0.4)
hats: s("hh*8").color('#63b4ff').postgain(0.2)
keys: n("0 2 4 7").scale("C minor").synth("Wavetable").color('gold').postgain(0.3).scope()
bass: note("c2 ~ c2 eb2").synth("Wavetable").color().postgain(0.3)
